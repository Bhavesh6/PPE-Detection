Three seconds at the gate

No new habits to learn, no forms, nothing to remember. A worker does what they already do: turn up.

  1. They badge in

    A card touch identifies who is at the gate. The screen greets them by name — not an ID number.

  2. The camera looks

    A vision model checks for a hardhat and a high-visibility vest, and holds the answer for a moment to be sure.

  3. The gate answers

    Cleared, and they are marked present. Missing something, and the screen names it so they can fix it and scan again.

It runs on the gate, not on a promise

The checkpoint decides on the hardware standing at the entrance. If the network goes, it keeps deciding, and files what it owes when the link returns.

Decides on the device
The model runs on the Raspberry Pi at the gate. No server in the loop, no round trip to wait on.
Keeps its own roster
Names, badges and the site's PPE policy are held locally, so a severed link costs a queue to drain — not a decision.
Sensors that join no network
Gas and temperature nodes reach the gate directly over ESP-NOW, and the gate carries them the rest of the way.
Refusals keep their evidence
Every refusal stores the frame that caused it, with the policy that was in force at that moment.

Try it with your own camera

The checkpoint runs in a browser. Stand in front of a webcam and it will tell you what you are missing.
