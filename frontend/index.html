Three seconds at the gate

No new habits to learn, no forms, nothing to remember. A worker does what they already do: turn up.

Cleared, and they are marked present. Missing something, and the screen names it so they can fix it and scan again.

One word, readable across the yard

These are real screens, not mock-ups — the detection boxes and the ruling come from the model itself.

It tells them what to fix

A refusal that just says “no” teaches nobody anything. This one names the missing item, in plain words, at a size readable from across the gate — then clears itself so the queue keeps moving.

Every decision is written down against a name, so a supervisor asked what controls were in place has an answer made of records rather than recollection. A refusal keeps the frame that produced it — the model's own boxes, burned in — so the answer isn't just a claim, it's a photograph.

It checks what the law already asks for

The BOCW Act, the Factories Act and the Occupational Safety Code already oblige an employer to supply protective equipment free of cost. Helmet, safety shoes and reflective jacket are the baseline on an Indian site, and the equipment must be ISI-certified to BIS standards.

Nothing here invents a policy. It verifies an existing one at the only moment that matters — the moment somebody walks in.

The decision

Badge scan identifies the worker, then the camera rules on their equipment. A pass has to hold for over a second, so one lucky frame cannot clear anybody. Entry is marked against a name, refusals included, with model speed shown live in frames per second.

Policy

Required equipment and detection confidence are settings, not constants, and a change takes effect on the next frame across all three surfaces. Only equipment the model can actually see is offered, because a requirement it could never satisfy would refuse everyone, permanently.

Evidence

Every refusal keeps the frame that caused it, with the model's own boxes drawn in, served through an authenticated route rather than as a public file. Images expire after thirty days; the decision they support does not. Operators can capture a frame on demand, kept out of the compliance figures.

Console

Overview, personnel, captures, reports, analytics, policy, location, change log and alerts — with per-worker scorecards, a daily trend, the hours refusals cluster in, and named CSV exports for any date range.

Hazard alerts

A critical alert overrides equipment compliance and pauses entry; full protective gear is still not safe in a gas leak. Raised by a device declaring a severity or a raw sensor value crossing a threshold, it surfaces on the gate display, the device and every open console tab, and clearing it is recorded against a person.

Accountability

An append-only log across policy, personnel and alerts. Names are kept alongside ids so the trail still reads after an account is deleted, and changes that weaken the gate are marked as such.

The device

A fullscreen gate application owning the camera and badge reader directly. Decisions that cannot reach the server are held locally and retried rather than lost, site position is reported from the device, and a pre-flight tool names the broken link instead of leaving a blank screen.

Interface

Rebuilt around one editorial design language shared by every page, then audited site-wide: four real bugs found where content overflowed with no way to scroll to it, and every page checked at phone, tablet and desktop widths.

Decisions we would defend

Refusals are photographed. Grants are not.
A checkpoint that photographs everyone is a surveillance system with a safety feature attached.
Identity comes from a badge, not a face.
A card answers the same question without putting a face database on a construction site.
Making the gate weaker is as visible as making it stronger.
Configurable policy without a log would make every compliance figure downstream unfalsifiable.
Any signed-in user can clear a hazard alert.
It is a safety action, not a policy edit. A gas alarm should not wait for an administrator to be reachable.
